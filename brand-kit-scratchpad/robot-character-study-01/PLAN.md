# The Human Touch · robot character study

## Execution plan

1. Explore ten human-scale humanoids in the scratchpad. Use the proportions of Greco-Roman sculpture, a human-shaped head with carved facial planes, and visible machine articulation. Compare how the surfaces catch daylight across pale, dark and warm mineral palettes. Keep blue out of this first round.
2. Compare the numbered sheets at full-figure and portrait scale. Select two or three candidates that read clearly on a projector. Choose the head, proportions and material treatment that should carry through the deck.
3. Place the shortlisted designs into the same two stills. In a painted interior, a woman begins to cry and the robot offers a kerchief. In a photography gallery, a subject raises a cigarette and the robot offers a light. A black-and-white James Dean portrait is one possible reference. Match perspective, contact shadows and the direction of light while retaining the robot’s photographic surface. Check recognition across eras and media.
4. Once the character is selected, make its definitive reference sheet. Record head profile, proportions, material finishes and distinguishing details. Promote the approved reference to `brand-kit-final/` and use it for all exhibit frames. The humanoid illustrates the platform’s scope. Identify the actual Maker Arm when showing the demonstration.
5. Build the pitch around the opening fresco and the ordinary “hold my beer” handoff. Choose each subsequent exhibit for the point it demonstrates. Give each encounter one readable human cue and one act of assistance. Include the connection between a real remote participant and a virtual robot, then show several sessions running at once.
6. Approve a static frame per exhibit, then use Higgsfield image-to-video with those frames. Check character identity, human-first timing and readability at presentation size. Rehearse with the selected clips and keep the approved stills available for playback.

## Imagegen prompts

Each design uses the shared prompt below, followed by its exact catalogue label and design direction.

Use case: product-mockup. Make a landscape three-column industrial-design character sheet, premium photographic product design, for The Human Touch. One original, beautiful machine: an approximately 1.73 m tall bipedal service humanoid. Body scaled like an idealized Greco-Roman statue, approximately seven and a half heads high, balanced weight, measured classical shoulder-to-hip ratios. The entire head has a HUMAN outer shape with a visible sculpted brow, nose bridge, cheekbones, lips and jaw, like a contemporary engineer worked from a carved classical portrait. Restrained expression, visibly manufactured stone or metal components, optical eyes, functioning neck/bearing and articulation. Photoreal manufactured object with physically accurate materials, fine craft, willingness to help; a photoreal visitor from our world, even in future paintings or photographic exhibits of different eras. Exactly three consistent photographic views left to right on the same seamless warm-grey ground: a frontal full-body standing view at left, a full-body rear three-quarter view in the center, a large front three-quarter portrait with head and upper chest on the right. Make both full figures equal height, include both feet and crown, same design and material in all views. Understated neutral pose and relaxed hands. Soft directional daylight upper left, clean catalogue lighting, good local contrast, shadows on the floor, natural perspective. Minimal background, no action and no artworks. No blue accents, glow, face visor, helmet, crown, helmet crest, drapery, Roman uniform, battle armor, biological skin, human hair, brand logos or hand studies. One small dark catalogue label at bottom right, exact text:
