The idea

When someone starts pouring from a heavy pitcher, a good partner steadies the bowl. Knowing when to reach, how close to stand and when to wait is what robots need to learn as they move into homes, workplaces and care settings. And the right answer changes from person to person.

So we built a rehearsal space where people teach robots how to work alongside them. A headset or a webcam brings a real person into Unreal Engine. Nine virtual tables rehearse the same moment in parallel with models of the MakerMods arms. Then real arms do it for real. We started with the simplest moment anyone can judge: handing someone a drink.

What we built

Five pieces, built in parallel by five people and their agents, all committing straight to main.

Real arms that hand you a drink

Two MakerMods Maker Arms on the table, driven three ways.

  • Our guarded controller picked up a cylinder and set it back on Saturday night.
  • The filmed take: the arm grips a water bottle, lifts it to a person and lets go as they take it.
  • Sunday night: Dream's model passed a bottle from one hand to the other, running on the Jetson.

Nine tables in Unreal

A shared Unreal world with the official Maker Arm model on a 3 × 3 grid of tables, plus Table 10 for people.

  • Dream-1 drove the virtual arms: grasp, lift, a two-arm handover and place back.
  • The handover-and-place task passed 9 of 9 tables, then 8 of 9 twice (grasps had help from a scripted reach and grip).
  • Simulation sped up from 11 to about 60 frames per second.

Real people inside the sim

Body pose from a webcam, and head and hands from a Quest headset, streamed live into Unreal.

  • MetaHumans and mannequins move with the performer.
  • The same poses mirrored into Isaac Sim for contact physics (4,369 checked).
  • In VR you just say it: "grab the can", "give it to the other arm", and the arms do it, including the real ones.

A brain at the edge

A Jetson Orin Nano at the station on its own travel-router network.

  • Live person, hand and bottle detection on a real webcam at about 30–37 ms a frame.
  • A small edge model trained in simulation, deciding in 0.038 ms.
  • Finally the home of Dream's own robot station, driving both arms and all three arm cameras.

One-click and one-command control

Ways for anyone on the team to drive the real arms safely.

  • A web panel: Turn arms on, Move up, Go to idle, Turn arms off, with the real arms within 2.6° of Unreal.
  • DreamSim/station.sh demo runs the whole Dream handover from a laptop.
  • A guide so teammates and their agents can reach the arms through the Jetson.

The story and the stage

A pitch built around a museum of human gestures, and a brand to carry it.

  • Judges, Stage and Investor decks with 4K exhibit films.
  • The nebula emblem with an ivory and titanium hand.
  • Submission copy that says exactly what was shown, and nothing more.

What we learned

The control loop matters as much as the model

Same model, same prompt: a slow loop that played one step at a time looked clumsy, and Dream's own 20 Hz station looked right. Run a robot model the way it was built to run.

One owner per adapter

Two USB-CAN adapters in one process made motors drop out. One process per arm fixed it, and it became a team rule.

Check the cameras, not just the robot

For a while Dream was steering blind on a frozen frame. Now stale frames are refused before they reach the model.

Say only what you showed

Our submission separates the filmed handover, VR control and assisted simulation, and claims no success rate we didn't measure.

What's next

Keep the bottle and vary the person: accept it, switch hands or pull away. Record which robot response each person preferred, then test whether a policy trained on those examples offers, waits or withdraws at the right moment.From our submission. On the robot side: a hybrid that keeps Dream for the plan and adds fast local control near the hand.
