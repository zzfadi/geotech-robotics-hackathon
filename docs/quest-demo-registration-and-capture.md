# Quest 3 demo station

## Register against the hardware

Use the two factory base plates as the room reference. The survey fits translation and yaw at unit scale. The table corners, floor and parked robot tool centers independently check the result. The [one-page operator diagram](../output/pdf/quest-arm-registration-guide.pdf) stays with the operator. Nothing needs to be printed or attached to the table.

1. **Identify the station.** Face across the table from the long edge carrying the arms. Choose the printed table number at launch. On the configured XR Mac, use `ht-rejoin --passthrough --table 10` for the dedicated interaction bay or `--table 0` for the original station. Other workstations can pass the same options to `Tools/join_human_touch.py` with their regular join arguments. Build the matching HumanTouchPose editor modules first. The project includes OpenXR hand tracking for the fingertip option.
2. **Check the physical references.** The pinned Maker Arm base flange measures 100 × 200 mm. The outer holes have 3.5 mm diameter at the upper face, with 180 mm between columns and 80 mm between the outer rows. Measure those two baselines on the installed flange. Use the same upper-face plane on both bases. A raised screw head or a tip inserted to the bottom of a bore changes the contact height. If the fitted hardware differs or the holes are covered, use a measured mechanical adapter with known offsets or update the surveyed datums before claiming a match. The CAD references come from `base_link`, solid `part_012`, and the fixed `link_007 → grasp_center` joint. [Pinned robot description](../Unreal/TheHumanTouch/SourceAssets/MakerArm/robot.urdf)
3. **Park the arms.** Disable motion during the entire survey. Match the physical and virtual joint poses. Place a stationary passive witness at each physical grasp center, using the arm's tool-center datum or a measured fixture. The wrist-to-grasp-center distance is 169.64 mm along negative local Z. Use a pose with the tool at least 12 cm horizontally away from its base axis so a backwards mount is detectable. The witness must remain accessible without reaching between powered jaws. If its position is unknown, full robot registration is unverified.
4. **Choose a pointer.** In **Tools → Capture Performers**, choose **Survey with right-controller probe** for the most repeatable contact. Fix a short, rigid, nonmarring pointer securely to the right Touch controller. Seat its physical tip in a fixed dimple, click **Capture marked point** and tilt the controller through different pitches and rolls while keeping the tip fixed. Ten distinct orientations estimate and check its tip offset. If using no accessory, choose **Survey with tracked fingertip**, put the controllers down and use the same bare right index fingertip throughout. Both inputs must pass the same station checks.
5. **Touch the features in the displayed order.** On each plate touch the near-left, near-right and far-left outer-hole centers. Use a centering point that registers at the specified upper-face plane. Then touch the four bare top corners in the order shown in the diagram (A near left, B near right, C far right, D far left). For rounded corners, locate the intersection of the two straight edge lines with a square. Touch the floor beside A, then the two parked tool witnesses. An operator clicks **Capture marked point** at each stop. Hold still through the acquisition. Finally change viewpoint and repeat the near-right hole on the left arm and near-left hole on the right arm.
6. **Read the result.** A passing survey places the local XR room once. A failed attempt reports the measurement that missed. The station stays at its original scale. Use **Recheck both mounts** after walking around the bench and immediately before each take. Re-register after recentering, a tracking-space change, a headset swap or moving a base. Keep the room textured and consistently lit so inside-out tracking has stationary features.

The console equivalents are `HumanTouch.Survey layout`, `start` (probe), `hand`, `capture`, `status`, `check` and `cancel`, each prefixed with `HumanTouch.Survey`. The existing five-second edge snap is available for a rough preview. A successful edge snap alone does not qualify the station for a measured take.

**Measure the mounting stack first.** In the saved map checked on November 7, the original and interaction stations both have a 200 × 80 cm tabletop, 75 cm above the virtual floor. The CAD flange upper faces are 15.7 mm below the tabletop. If your flanges sit on top of the physical surface, the bench and model have different geometry. Measure the signed flange-top to tabletop offset, tabletop dimensions and both base locations before the rehearsal. Use the survey receipt to correct the station model and repeat the independent checks. A global height adjustment cannot correct two different relative heights.

### What qualifies a take

| Check | Demo threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Probe tip pivot | Eight poses for the solve, two withheld. Maximum pivot error 3 mm |
| Stable touch | Five samples over roughly one second, within a 4 mm neighborhood |
| Fixed base points | All six within 5 mm and the L1–R1 span within 5 mm. Two holes are withheld from the four-point fit |
| Mount direction | Each long bolt row within 2° |
| Table footprint | All four edges and both diagonals within 1% and 10 mm |
| Independent surface, floor and tool checks | Corners and both parked tool centers within 10 mm in 3D. Floor elevation within 10 mm |
| Second viewpoint | Both repeated contacts within 5 mm of their first samples. The pre-take recheck tests both against the registered model within 5 mm |

The room transform uses gravity for roll and pitch, so a tilted or uneven setup can fail the checks. A passing stationary pose establishes accuracy at those measured features. A moving robot additionally requires matching encoder-to-joint calibration and synchronized feedback. This workflow moves the XR room locally and sends no robot commands.

The local receipts live under `Saved/Capture/CornerSurveys/`. Use the measured table length, depth and floor-to-top height, the two flange centers relative to A (across, right, up), and signed point errors to correct the modeled station if it fails. Similar errors across all table corners with good base checks point to the modeled tabletop; a group of errors on one side points to that mount or pose. If both mounts, both tool points and repeat touches agree but the tabletop fails, the receipt also gives `surveyed_tabletop_center_world_cm`, `surveyed_table_forward_yaw_deg` and `surveyed_floor_world_z_cm`. These are bench measurements expressed in the robot-anchored world frame. For a model correction, place the tabletop mesh center half its thickness below that measured top center, set its two horizontal dimensions to the measured depth and length, and match the supporting legs to the measured floor. Check dependent fixtures, camera and simulator references when changing the shared layout. If one base or tool fails, investigate its mounting transform or joint configuration first. Recheck the physical dimensions with a tape or calipers before editing shared geometry. Keep the robot link meshes and Unreal world scale at 1:1. The headset tracks motion between observations; the system does not continuously recognize the base plates through the room cameras.

## Share the mixed-reality view live

Use the output of the **Quest compositor**. The Unreal desktop viewport and the outgoing OXRSys video contain the virtual foreground. The headset adds the physical room. On the demo Mac, Meta Quest Developer Hub (MVDH) and OBS are installed.

1. Connect the Quest 3 to the Mac with a data cable, enable Developer Mode, and accept USB debugging in the headset if prompted. Leave OXRSys open in the verified Luna2 session. In MVDH **Device Manager → Device Actions → Cast**, start with **Original (1:1)** for the full single-eye framing. **Cropped (16:9)** is available once both arms and the tabletop fit inside that crop. For wireless casting, enable **ADB over Wi-Fi** in MVDH on the same network. Wired casting is the preferred rehearsal route when OXRSys is already using the venue Wi-Fi. [Meta casting controls](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/spatial-sdk/ts-mqdh-media/)
2. Have a second person watch the **cast window**. Pan slowly past a real, distinctive item beside one fixed arm and a distinctly virtual object. Confirm those features and the table edge remain visible together on the audience display. Meta has shipped a fix for Quest 3 casting showing a black background despite passthrough working in-headset. Check the exact headset, viewer build and Mac at rehearsal. [Meta's Quest 3 passthrough casting investigation](https://developers.meta.com/vr/support/investigations/2134506760683039/)
3. For a projector, put the cast window on the audience display. For a video call, share just that window. If using OBS, add **macOS Screen Capture → Window Capture** and select the MVDH cast window. Check the projector or remote attendee's view. If casting drops or the room is black, play the locally embedded clip. [OBS macOS window capture](https://obsproject.com/kb/macos-screen-capture-source)

## Record the pitch clip first

1. On Quest 3, open **Camera → Settings** and choose landscape **1920 × 1080**, **36 fps**, **20 Mbps** and low stabilization. Open OXRSys, start recording from the headset Camera control, and hold the first shot steady. MVDH also supports single-eye recording from **Device Manager → Record**. On Mac its local captures are in `~/Library/Application Support/odh/captures/`. Headset-native files can be copied with MVDH **File Manager** from `Oculus/VideoShots`. [Meta's mixed-reality recording guide](https://developers.meta.com/vr/resources/video-capture-mr-vr/) · [MVDH recording options](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/spatial-sdk/ts-mqdh-media/)
2. Record a 15–25 second take. Start wide with the real bench and both arms, move sideways to show the overlay holding position at the base, then hold on the task or tool. Play the file **on the presentation laptop** and check that the room and virtual content were actually recorded. Keep the original.
3. From the repository root, export the useful part as a silent, single-eye 1080p H.264 MP4. The helper preserves framing and pads a square source. The example keeps 15 seconds beginning two seconds into the source.

   ```sh
   python3 Capture/prepare_pitch_clip.py "$HOME/Movies/quest-take.mp4" "$HOME/Movies/pitch-table.mp4" --start 2 --seconds 15
   ```

4. Drag the MP4 onto the Keynote slide or use **Insert → Video** in PowerPoint (choose **Movie from File** on Mac or **This Device** on Windows). Run the slide show with networking off and test the same HDMI/projector path intended for the pitch. Keep the standalone MP4 beside the deck as a second playback path. [PowerPoint video embedding](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/insert-and-play-a-video-file-from-your-computer)
