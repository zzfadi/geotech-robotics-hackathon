## 4. The Human Touch · 12 s remaining

A can is easy to describe. The hard part is knowing what the person needs next. If I lift one end of a box, the useful place for a robot to be is at the other end. That’s what we mean by the human touch: two different actions that get one job done.

## 5. The missing interaction · 19 s

A robotics team can make a thousand copies of a kitchen. Who tips the jug differently on the next run? Our idea is to let a real person enter each virtual scene, change the timing, and tell us whether the robot helped. The kitchen is simulated. The person making the decision is real.

## 10. Real people in virtual rehearsal · 20 s

A person can join through a webcam or headset. Their motion drives an avatar in Unreal, alongside a virtual robot. Isaac is the physics layer in our design. Separate rooms can run at once, so different people can rehearse the same interaction without waiting for a physical arm.

## 11. Evidence · what we ran · 20 s

We ran ten simulations in parallel. Dream made 84 attempts in 170 seconds. On the physical station, the right arm picked up a cylinder, lifted it, placed it and parked in 16 seconds using replayed waypoints. Those are separate runs. [If a repeat trial is locked, show it live and shorten this narration.]

## 12. What failed · 12 s

One simulated cup stayed in the gripper but stopped below its target height. We counted that as a miss. A robot can hold on and still fall short of what the person asked it to do.

## 13. What comes next · 13 s

Next we’ll let a person interrupt a rehearsal and rate the response. Then we’ll try the same cue on the shared station and measure whether the arm responds at the right moment.

## 14. Close · project link · 7 s

Bring us one task that takes two people. Let’s rehearse what the robot should do on the other side.
